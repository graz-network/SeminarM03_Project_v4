# Report structure for the seminar paper

## Non-negotiable output requirements
- Final deliverable: scientific report exported to PDF.
- Maximum length: 15 pages.
- Use figures and tables as evidence, not decoration.
- Keep the replication and extension clearly separated, then synthesize them in the discussion.

## Recommended section order
1. Abstract (150-250 words)
2. Introduction
3. Literature Review
4. Replication Study Design
5. Replication Results
6. Results (extension section)
7. Discussion and Implications
8. Conclusion
9. References

## What each section should do

### Abstract
Must cover:
- background and motivation;
- replication objectives;
- extension objectives;
- methods and data;
- key findings;
- conclusion and implications.

### Introduction
Must establish:
- that the paper is a replication study;
- why replication matters here;
- the original paper's key contribution;
- your own contribution in both replication and extension form;
- the structure of the report.

### Literature Review
Should position:
- the original paper in the literature;
- related work on women in politics, voter bias, demonstration effects, quotas, and local elections;
- the marginal contribution of your replication and extension.

### Replication Study Design
Should explain:
- what is replicated exactly;
- the datasets and sample;
- the empirical design;
- expected degree of match with the original;
- data or identification limitations.

### Replication Results
Should include:
- reproduced main tables and figures;
- comparison to the paper;
- discussion of any discrepancies;
- robustness or sensitivity checks.

### Results (extension)
Should include:
- motivation for the extension;
- the new research question;
- any new data construction or new estimands;
- extension results and robustness checks.

### Discussion and Implications
Should interpret:
- what the replication taught you about the original claims;
- what the extension adds;
- limitations;
- policy or research implications.

### Conclusion
Should close with:
- whether the original results replicated;
- what the extension contributed;
- realistic next steps for future work.

## Writing advice built into this skill
- Keep the narrative economical; do not waste page budget repeating tables already visible.
- Use one paragraph to explain the institutional setting clearly, then move quickly to design and evidence.
- Label every extension claim with the exact identifying choice behind it.
